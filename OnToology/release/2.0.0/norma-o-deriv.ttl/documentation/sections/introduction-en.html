Introduction back to ToC

Version 2.0 of NORMA-O separates what is asserted about norms from what is derived from them. An annotation graph states norms, their conditions, defeaters and cases. A derived graph states what follows: the deontic closure of each norm, the legal positions it confers, the relations between norms, the prevalence ordering, and, for each case, which norms apply, to whom, and why not when they do not.

Derived conclusions differ from annotations in two ways. First, they are the output of a computation, so they must be regenerated whenever the annotations change, never edited by hand and never merged back into the annotation graph. Second, they rest on readings that the annotations do not fix: whether a norm missing from the graph is missing from the legal system, whether a condition the case is silent about holds, or which semantics resolves conflicting defeats. Two producers can reach different conclusions from the same norms if they read them differently.

This module provides the vocabulary to state both: the provenance of a derived graph, and the assumptions under which its conclusions hold. It is deliberately small and independent of any particular implementation. NORMA-O ships a reference rule set that writes its results with this vocabulary, but any producer may do so with its own tools.

The module imports NORMA-O and PROV-O. Its classes specialise prov:Activity, prov:Entity and skos:Concept, and its three value classes come with their own SKOS concept schemes.
