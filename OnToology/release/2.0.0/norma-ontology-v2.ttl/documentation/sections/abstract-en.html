Abstract

NORMA-O is an OWL 2 DL ontology for representing legal norms as machine-readable, interoperable RDF graphs. It describes what a norm requires, forbids, allows or recommends; under which conditions it applies; whom it addresses; which action and object it regulates; and on which legal source it rests. Every statement remains traceable to its legal source and to the annotation process that produced it, with its method, review status and lifecycle.

Version 2.0 extends the model from describing individual norms to reasoning over a body of norms. It grounds the deontic types in Standard Deontic Logic and adds action negation, the deontic closure of each norm, weak permission, and equivalence, contradiction and conflict between norms; Hohfeldian legal positions (duty, claim, privilege and no-right) held by one party toward another; legal roles, concrete parties and described cases; defeasibility through exceptions, conflicts resolved by lex superior, specialis and posterior, and repeal, together with a hierarchy of sources and a prevalence graph; and counts-as chaining, through which constitutive rules establish the institutional facts on which regulative norms depend.

What follows from the annotations is kept apart from the annotations themselves. The companion module NORMA-O Derivation (normad) describes derived graphs, the activity and rule set that produced them and the assumptions under which each conclusion holds, so that every result can be traced, reproduced and questioned. NORMA-O reuses PROV-O, ELI, FOAF and SKOS, keeps every term of version 1.0, and is part of ongoing research on the formal representation of legal norms.
